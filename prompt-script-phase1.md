# PHASE 1 — ADAPTIVE MASTER SCRIPT PROMPT

### **INPUT**

VIDEO TOPIC:  
\[INSERT VIDEO TOPIC\]

PROFESSION:  
\[INSERT PROFESSION\]

TARGET LANGUAGE:  
English unless another language is requested.

TARGET DURATION:  
\[INSERT DURATION\]

TARGET WORD COUNT:  
Adapt to the requested duration. For 17–20 minutes, target approximately 2,600–2,800 words.

### **FIRST TASK: BUILD A PROFESSION PROFILE PRIVATELY**

Before writing, identify the profession’s real work ecosystem:

* typical entry route and minimum requirements;  
* genuine job titles and seniority steps;  
* daily duties and difficult assignments;  
* tools, machines, protective equipment and technology;  
* work locations, weather, schedules and travel;  
* physical, technical, legal and safety risks;  
* training, licenses, exams and recurring qualifications;  
* realistic compensation model: salary, overtime, per job, contract, commission or business profit;  
* major expenses, debt, insurance and equipment costs;  
* employers, clients, unions, regulators or public authorities;  
* routes to specialization, supervision, management, ownership or exit;  
* effects on sleep, health, family, relationships, identity and freedom.

Select only the details that genuinely belong to the chosen profession. If the topic is a special case—such as commercial pilot, surgeon, military soldier, nuclear worker, real estate agent or cybersecurity analyst—replace the usual manual-labor progression with the correct professional pathway.

### **ADAPTIVE STORY ARCHITECTURE**

Choose 7–9 meaningful levels based on the profession profile. The levels may include, when relevant:

* curious beginner or student;  
* apprentice, trainee or junior worker;  
* licensed or independently employable professional;  
* experienced field operator;  
* specialist in difficult or high-value work;  
* senior, supervisor, crew leader or instructor;  
* elite expert, manager, contractor or business owner;  
* late-career transition, injury, burnout, retirement or final perspective.

Do not use a level merely because it appears in this list. Every level must represent a real change in competence, authority, income, risk, working conditions or personal cost.

For each level, integrate naturally into the narration:

* approximate age and professional status;  
* realistic compensation range and how it is earned;  
* work environment and schedule;  
* profession-specific tools or systems;  
* one concrete task or decision;  
* one obstacle, danger, ethical tension or uncomfortable truth;  
* why the person advances or changes direction;  
* emotional and personal consequence.

### **NARRATIVE REQUIREMENTS**

Write primarily in the second person using “you”. Make the viewer live the profession through concrete scenes rather than abstract explanations.

Open inside a specific moment involving a young person entering the profession. Within the first 100 words include an age, location, professional object or machine, sensory detail, emotional tension and a sharp contrast with the future—authority, responsibility, achievement or income.

After the opening, state the promise of the documentary and add a short, natural subscription invitation.

Alternate throughout the script between:

Story → explanation → technical detail → financial reality → personal consequence → story.

Use open loops, mini-cliffhangers, unexpected costs, salary contrasts, callbacks and profession-specific revelations. Avoid repetitive transitions, generic motivation, exaggerated tragedy and empty phrases.

### **FACTUAL INTEGRITY**

Use realistic ranges rather than false precision. Distinguish clearly between employee pay, overtime, contract income, commission, business revenue and personal profit. Mention when figures vary by country, region, company, experience or specialization.

For current or country-specific facts, verify reliable sources before presenting them. Prefer government labor statistics, official regulators, professional associations, training institutions, unions, company reports and reputable industry publications. Never invent experts, quotes, regulations or statistics. Add a concise source note after the script without interrupting the narration.

### **ENDING**

Return visually to the opening beginner or to the same first-day object, location or sound. Contrast what the career provided—income, authority, expertise, security or independence—with what it demanded—time, health, relationships, freedom, peace or identity. End with one original, memorable contrast specific to the profession.

### **OUTPUT FORMAT**

Write only the complete spoken narration.

No chapter headings, bullet points, timestamps or production notes.

Use one spoken idea per line and no blank lines.
