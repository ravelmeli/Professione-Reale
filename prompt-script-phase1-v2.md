# PHASE 1 — MASTER SCRIPT PROMPT (UNIVERSAL VERSION)

Channel format: **"La tua vita a ogni livello come [PROFESSIONE]"**
Market: Italy. Spoken language: Italian.

---

## INPUT

PROFESSION (Italian name + English name):
[es. Elettricista (Electrician)]

VIDEO TITLE:
La tua vita a ogni livello come [professione in italiano]

TARGET LANGUAGE:
Italian (native, spoken, natural; never a translation feel).

COUNTRY / MARKET:
Italy (default). If the profession's best-known pathway is abroad (e.g. commercial pilot with international airlines, US-style lineman), state the country explicitly in the narration and give the Italian equivalent when it exists.

TARGET DURATION:
[es. 17–20 minutes]

TARGET WORD COUNT:
Spoken Italian ≈ 150 words per minute. 17–20 min ≈ 2,600–3,000 words. Always compute from the requested duration; never pad or cut to hit a number if the story needs a different length within ±10%.

---

## STEP 0 — CLASSIFY THE PROFESSION (PRIVATE, BEFORE WRITING)

Choose the pathway archetype that matches the profession. The archetype decides how the levels, the money and the promotion mechanism are told. Never force one profession into another's ladder.

| Archetype | Typical professions | How progression works | How money works |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Trade with apprenticeship | HVAC, electrician, plumber, elevator mechanic, lineman, solar installer, wind turbine technician | apprentice → qualified → independent → specialist → foreman → owner | CCNL salary (lordo/netto), overtime, trasferte, then own-business revenue vs profit |
| B. Site / industrial operator | construction worker, crane operator, refinery worker, nuclear plant worker, railroad worker, miner | entry → certified operator → senior/specialist → supervisor | CCNL levels, indennità (turni, rischio, altezza, trasferta), overtime |
| C. Licensed / regulated career | commercial pilot, surgeon, real estate agent | school/exam/licence gates, then seniority or specialization | training debt, early low pay, then steep jump; commission or contract for agents |
| D. Rank-based public service | police officer, firefighter, military soldier | concorso → real ranks (gradi) → promotion by anzianità / merit / exam | public pay scale, indennità, straordinari, pension rules |
| E. Contract / freelance mobility | truck driver, commercial fisherman, commercial diver, logger | employee → experienced → specialist → owner-operator | per-trip / per-day / share-of-catch; costs of vehicle, boat, gear |
| F. Service / low-barrier ladder | garbage collector, restaurant worker | entry → skilled → lead → manager or owner | CCNL, tips/extras where legal, owner profit vs risk |
| G. Knowledge / technical track | cybersecurity analyst, mining engineer | junior → analyst/engineer → senior → lead → principal/manager or consultant | RAL, bonus, on-call, certifications as cost |

If a profession fits two archetypes (e.g. a diver can be employee or contractor), say so in the narration at the exact level where the fork happens.

## STEP 1 — BUILD THE PROFESSION PROFILE (PRIVATE)

Before writing, identify the real work ecosystem and keep only what truly belongs to this profession:

* entry route, minimum age, minimum education, medical or legal requirements;
* real Italian job titles, levels (livelli CCNL) or ranks (gradi), and the real name of every licence, patentino, abilitazione or exam;
* daily duties and the most difficult assignments;
* tools, machines, protective equipment, technology, and their real names;
* locations, weather, shifts, travel, nights, weekends;
* physical, technical, legal and safety risks; recurring medicals, retraining and renewals;
* compensation model (see Step 0) and **the exact type of figure** you will quote: lordo annuo (RAL), netto mensile, tariffa oraria, fatturato, utile;
* major costs: training, equipment, insurance, vehicle or boat, tax regime (dipendente, partita IVA forfettaria, ditta individuale, società);
* employers, clients, unions, regulators and public authorities (e.g. INAIL, INPS, ENAC, Ministero, Ordini);
* routes out: specialization, supervision, management, ownership, abroad, retraining, early retirement;
* effects on sleep, health, family, relationships, identity and freedom.

---

## STEP 2 — ADAPTIVE STORY ARCHITECTURE

Choose **7–9 levels** (fewer only if the profession genuinely has fewer real steps, never fewer than 6). Each level must mark a real change in at least one of: competence, authority, income, risk, working conditions or personal cost.

Possible level types, used only when they exist in that profession: curious beginner or student; apprentice or trainee; first licensed or independently employable stage; experienced operator; specialist in difficult or high-value work; senior, supervisor or instructor; elite expert, manager, contractor or owner; late-career transition, injury, burnout, retirement or final perspective.

For every level, weave naturally into the narration:

1. approximate age and status;
2. realistic pay range **and how it is earned** (and the type of figure: lordo, netto, fatturato, utile);
3. environment and schedule;
4. profession-specific tools or systems;
5. one concrete task or decision;
6. one obstacle, danger, ethical tension or uncomfortable truth;
7. why the person advances or changes direction;
8. the emotional and personal consequence.

Rhythm rule: every level gets a different emphasis. One level leads with danger, another with money, another with a mistake, another with a relationship cost. Never run all levels through the same eight-step template in the same order.

---

## STEP 3 — NARRATIVE REQUIREMENTS

* Primarily second person (**"tu"**). The viewer lives the career through concrete scenes, not abstract explanations.
* **Opening (first 100 words):** drop into one specific moment of a young person entering the profession. Include an age, a place, a professional object or machine, a sensory detail, an emotional tension and a sharp contrast with the future (authority, responsibility, achievement or income). No greeting, no "in questo video".
* After the opening, state the promise of the documentary in one or two lines, then add one short, natural invitation to subscribe.
* Alternate throughout: story → explanation → technical detail → financial reality → personal consequence → story.
* Use open loops, mini-cliffhangers, unexpected costs, salary contrasts, callbacks and profession-specific revelations. Place a hook line at roughly every 60–90 seconds of spoken text.
* Avoid repetitive transitions, generic motivation, exaggerated tragedy, clichés ("sudore e sacrificio"), and empty phrases.
* **Technical vocabulary:** use the real Italian insider terms. When a term is unfamiliar, explain it in the same sentence through a concrete example, never with a definition-style interruption.
* **Spoken Italian:** short and medium sentences, natural rhythm for a voice recording, no sentence that cannot be said in one breath, no stacked subordinate clauses, no hard-to-pronounce acronym strings without a spoken form. Write numbers as they should be read aloud (es. "mille e duecento euro al mese", "ventotto mila euro lordi l'anno").

---

## STEP 4 — FACTUAL INTEGRITY (STRICT)

* Use realistic **ranges**, never false precision. Always say which type of figure it is (lordo / netto / fatturato / utile) and when it varies by region, company, contract, experience or specialization.
* Keep employee pay, overtime and allowances, contract income, commission, business revenue and personal profit clearly separate.
* For current or country-specific facts, verify reliable sources before stating them. Prefer: CCNL texts, ISTAT, INPS, INAIL, Ministero del Lavoro, ENAC, Ministero della Difesa / Interno, bandi di concorso, Ordini professionali, sindacati, enti di formazione, bilanci aziendali, reputable industry publications; for foreign pathways, the equivalent official source.
* Never invent experts, quotes, regulations, statistics, anecdotes or "insider" statements. If a figure cannot be verified, give a clearly qualified range or leave it out.
* Never copy sentences, anecdotes, statistics, scenes or distinctive phrasing from another channel or source. Original wording only.
* Safety and legality: no instructions that would help someone bypass safety rules, licences or regulations.

---

## STEP 5 — ENDING

Return to the opening beginner, or to the same first-day object, place or sound. Contrast what the career gave (income, authority, expertise, security, independence) with what it demanded (time, health, relationships, freedom, peace, identity). Finish on one original, memorable contrast that belongs only to this profession. No generic moral, no call to action after the last line.

One spoken idea per line, no blank lines.

## SELF-CHECK BEFORE DELIVERING (PRIVATE, DO NOT PRINT)

* The pathway archetype fits the profession, with no generic ladder forced onto it.
* 7–9 levels, each a real change; each level emphasizes something different.
* Every pay figure states what type it is and how it is earned.
* The first 100 words contain age, place, object, sensory detail, tension and contrast.
* A hook roughly every 60–90 seconds of speech.
* Real insider terms used and explained through context.
* No invented source, quote or statistic; uncertain numbers are flagged.
* The text reads aloud naturally in Italian; numbers written for speaking.
* The ending returns to the opening image and lands one contrast specific to this profession.
* Word count matches the duration at about 150 words per minute (±10%).
